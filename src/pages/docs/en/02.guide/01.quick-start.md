# Quick Start

This is the full roadmap for someone using the platform for the first time. You do not need to understand containers or Kubernetes first — if you can use a mouse and copy-paste, you can finish it.

The whole trip has five stages. **Finish one stage before starting the next.** Every stage tells you four things: what the goal is, exactly what to click, what you will see when you are done, and which page to read if you get stuck.

:::tip Three sentences to remember

- A **cluster** is the building that holds the machines, and a **workspace** is your office inside it. Everything you do happens inside some workspace.
- Deploying anything follows the same flow: **pick a template → fill in parameters → click Confirm**.
- When the status changes from **Installed** to **Healthy**, the thing is running and ready to use.

:::

## Stage 1: Sign in and learn the interface

### Goal

Get into the AI Platform successfully and recognise three key positions on the page: the product switcher, the left menu, and the workspace selector.

### How to do it

1. Type the platform address into your browser's address bar (ask your platform admin for it; it usually ends with `/console`).
2. Fill in **Username** and **Password** on the sign-in form and click **Login**.
3. If a captcha appears, follow the prompt — drag the slider, or type the characters shown in the image.
4. If multi-factor authentication is enabled for your account, a second code box appears. Open the authenticator app on your phone and enter the current 6-digit code.
5. If you belong to more than one tenant, a tenant list appears; click the tenant you want to enter. If you belong to only one, this step is skipped.
6. Once you are in the console, recognise three positions:

   | Position | Where it is | What it does |
   | --- | --- | --- |
   | Products | Top-left corner of the page | Switch between the AI Platform, Moha Hub and AIrouter |
   | Left menu | Left side of the page | Go to Inference, Fine-tuning, Storage and other features |
   | Workspace selector | Top of the left menu | Switch the cluster and workspace you are working in |

### What you will see

The **Home** page appears, with cards such as "Total workloads", "Running / healthy" and "Quota warnings". If Home says "Select a region and workspace to view overview data", Stage 2 is not done yet.

### Stuck? read this

- Sign-in, captcha, forgotten password: [Sign In](/account/auth/login)
- Second verification code: [Multi-factor Authentication](/account/auth/mfa)
- Choosing a tenant: [Select and Register a Tenant](/account/auth/select-tenant)

## Stage 2: Get compute (pick a cluster and workspace)

### Goal

Pick one workspace under some cluster. Quota is allocated per workspace, so without a workspace you cannot deploy anything.

### How to do it

1. At the top of the left menu, in the workspace selector, pick the **Cluster** first, then the **Workspace**.
2. If the dropdown has no workspace in it, nobody has created one yet. Create it first:

   1. Click **Create Workspace**.
   2. Choose the **Cluster** it belongs to (usually filled in from what you picked in the selector).
   3. Enter a **Name**, for example `my-first-workspace`.
   4. Enter a **Description** (you may leave it empty).
   5. Check the generated **ID**. The rules are: it starts with a lowercase letter, it may contain only lowercase letters, digits and hyphens, and it is at most 63 characters. A Chinese name is converted to pinyin automatically. The ID is normally not changed after creation; if you do need to change it, click the pencil icon next to it and edit it by hand.
   6. Click **Confirm**.

3. Go back to Home and confirm the "Select a region and workspace" message is gone.

:::warning Not enough quota means creation fails

If creation reports "No resource quota is configured for this tenant in this cluster, so the workspace cannot be created.", the tenant has no quota on this cluster yet. Ask a tenant admin or a platform admin to allocate it — see [Quota](/rune/console/quota).

:::

### What you will see

The top of the left menu shows the cluster and workspace you picked. The **Resource Quota** area on Home shows the CPU, memory and accelerator limits and how much is used.

### Stuck? read this

- What a workspace is and how to manage it: [Workspace](/rune/console/workspace)
- Never enough quota: [Quota](/rune/console/quota)
- Not sure what "Flavor" or "accelerator" means: [Glossary](/guide/glossary)

## Stage 3: Run your first inference service

### Goal

Deploy one of the platform's existing inference templates as an online service and confirm it reaches **Healthy**. This is the heart of the whole flow.

### How to do it

1. Confirm the cluster and workspace are selected at the top of the left menu.
2. Click **Inference** in the left menu, then click **Create Inference** in the top-right corner.
3. In the template list, use the category tabs at the top to filter, choose **Inference**, then click the template card you want to use.
4. On the template detail page, click **Deploy** to open the deployment form. The page title reads "Deploy / template name".
5. On the template information card, choose a **Version**. If the dropdown has several, prefer the one marked **Latest Version**.
6. Fill in **Basic Info**:

   | Field | How to fill it |
   | --- | --- |
   | Name | The display name of this service; required |
   | ID | Generated from the name, with the same rules as a workspace; click the pencil icon next to it if you need to change it |
   | Description | May be left empty |

7. In the **Parameters** card, fill in the template parameters. Different templates have different fields; the common ones are model path, replicas, max sequence length and quantization method.

   - The default is **form mode**, and every field has an explanation next to it.
   - Click the code icon in the top-right corner to **switch to the JSON editor**, which is handy when you want to change several parameters at once. Switch back when you are done.
   - If this template needs compute, a card titled **Flavor** appears among the parameters; tick one row. The table shows Name, Type, CPU and GPU.
   - **AI Config Guide** in the top-right corner explains the current configuration items. Use it when you are unsure how to fill something in.

8. When everything looks right, click **Confirm** at the bottom of the page.

:::warning If no flavor can be selected, it is usually quota

If the **Flavor** card has no selectable rows, or it warns about insufficient resources, the current workspace usually does not have enough quota. Ask a tenant admin to adjust the quota, or stop other services you are not using to free resources.

:::

### What you will see

The page goes back to the inference list with a new service in it:

1. **Runtime Status** first shows **Installed**, meaning the containers are starting; after a moment it becomes **Healthy**.
2. Open that service and you will see pages such as **Overview**, **Monitoring**, **Log**, and **Events**.
3. **Overview** shows the access address, the resource spec and the creation time.
4. **Log** shows the service's startup output; **Events** shows image pulls, scheduling and similar steps.

### Stuck? read this

- Deployment failed, or the status stays on **Needs attention** or **Processing failed**: [Inference](/rune/console/inference)
- You want a shell inside the container or an SSH connection: [Runebox](/rune/console/devenv)
- A term is unclear: [Glossary](/guide/glossary)

## Stage 4: Upload your own model

### Goal

Put your own trained model files into the platform so the inference service from Stage 3 can reference them. There are two ways to do it — pick one.

### Way A: put them in storage (good for large files and model weights)

> Creating and deleting storage volumes needs the **Tenant Admin** role. If you cannot see the **Create Storage Volume** button, use Way B instead, or ask a tenant admin to create it for you.

1. Click **Storage** in the left menu.
2. Click **Create Storage Volume**.
3. Fill in the form:

   | Field | How to fill it |
   | --- | --- |
   | Name | Lowercase letters, digits and hyphens; must start and end with a letter or digit |
   | Storage Cluster | Pick the storage your cluster provides |
   | Storage Class | Pick an available storage class |
   | Capacity | For example `20Gi`; leave enough room for the model |
   | Access Mode | Pick `ReadWriteOnce`, `ReadOnlyMany` or `ReadWriteMany` as needed |
   | Description | May be left empty |

4. Click **Create** and wait for "Storage volume created successfully".
5. Open the storage volume and upload the model files in the **File Manager**.
6. If the model is on HuggingFace, ModelScope or Moha Hub, you do not have to upload it by hand: inside the storage volume click **Create Storage Job**, choose the matching kind and repository address, and the platform pulls the files in for you in the background.

### Way B: put them in Moha Hub (good for models you version long-term)

1. Click **Products** in the top-left corner and switch to Moha Hub.
2. Click **Models** at the top.
3. Click **Create Model**, fill in the model name, visibility and similar fields, and submit.
4. Inside the model repository, upload files, or push them with Git commands.
5. Later, when you deploy an inference service in the AI Platform, simply reference this model in the parameters.

### What you will see

- Way A: the storage volume appears in the **Storage** list, and the files you uploaded appear in its **File Manager**.
- Way B: the model appears under **Models** in Moha Hub, with version history.

### Stuck? read this

- Storage volumes and storage jobs: [Storage](/rune/console/storage)
- Model management in Moha Hub: [Models](/moha/models)
- The full upload and download reference: [Uploading Models](/moha/models/upload)

## Stage 5: Fine-tuning and evaluation

### Goal

"Top up" an existing model with your own data (fine-tuning), then score the result (evaluation). Both use the same deployment flow as Stage 3, just with a different template.

### Fine-tuning

1. Click **Traning&Fine tuning** in the left menu, then click **Create Jobs**.
2. In the template list, choose a training job template under the **Fine-tuning** category and click **Deploy**.
3. Choose a version, then fill in Name, ID and Description.
4. In **Parameters**, fill in the training parameters. The common ones are base model, training dataset, learning rate, number of epochs and flavor.
5. Click **Confirm**. The job appears in the list, first as **Installed**, then as **Running**.
6. Open the job detail and check **Monitoring** for training metrics such as loss, learning rate, accuracy and the current epoch. When training finishes, the status becomes **Succeeded**.

### Evaluation

1. Click **Evaluations** in the left menu, then click **Create Evaluations**.
2. Choose an evaluation engine template and click **Deploy**.
3. Choose a version, fill in Name, ID and Description, and specify the model and the evaluation dataset in the parameters.
4. Click **Confirm** and wait for the job to finish.

:::tip Where fine-tuning results go

The model files produced by fine-tuning normally land in a storage volume first. You can then register them into Moha Hub as a versioned model for later deployment.

:::

### What you will see

- The **Traning&Fine tuning** list shows the job going from **Running** to **Succeeded**.
- The job detail shows training curves such as loss.
- The **Evaluations** list shows the evaluation job and its results.

### Stuck? read this

- The full fine-tuning parameter reference: [Fine-tuning](/rune/console/finetune)
- How to configure an evaluation: [Evaluations](/rune/console/evaluation)
- Comparing experiment results: [Metrics](/rune/console/experiment)
